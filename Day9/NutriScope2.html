Educational Disclaimer

NutriScope is a learning and self-awareness tool, not a clinical or diagnostic instrument. The calorie and nutrient targets shown are population-level estimates generated from standard formulas and general adult recommended dietary allowances (RDAs) — they are not tailored to your individual physiology, lab values, medications, allergies, pregnancy or lactation status, or any diagnosed condition. Food nutrient values are drawn from generalized composition averages and will vary from the specific items you actually eat based on brand, preparation method, ripeness, and portion estimation.

Nothing in this app constitutes medical, dietetic, or clinical advice. Do not use NutriScope to diagnose a nutrient deficiency, adjust medication, manage a medical condition, or replace guidance from a registered dietitian, physician, or other qualified health professional. If you suspect a real deficiency or health issue, please consult a professional and consider lab-based testing rather than relying on self-logged estimates.

Calculation Methodology
Basal Metabolic Rate: Mifflin-St Jeor equation, the most widely validated BMR formula for general adult populations.
Total Energy: BMR × activity multiplier (1.2–1.9 depending on self-reported activity level).
Macro split: 20% protein / 50% carbohydrate / 30% fat of total energy — a standard balanced distribution, not personalized to training goals (e.g. muscle gain or fat loss).
Micronutrient targets: general adult RDAs, adjusted only by gender where standard guidance differs (e.g. iron, vitamin A, magnesium, zinc).
Sodium is tracked as an upper limit (don't exceed) rather than a target to reach, reflecting its role as a risk nutrient rather than a deficiency nutrient.
Nutrition Data Sources
Food composition values approximate figures published by the Indian Council of Medical Research – National Institute of Nutrition (ICMR-NIN) food composition tables and the USDA FoodData Central database for items without Indian-specific data.
Adult RDA figures are based on the general ranges published by ICMR-NIN dietary guidelines and the U.S. National Academies / NIH Office of Dietary Supplements for nutrients not covered by Indian guidance.
BMR formula: Mifflin MD, St Jeor ST, et al., "A new predictive equation for resting energy expenditure in healthy individuals," American Journal of Clinical Nutrition, 1990.
All figures are reference approximations for educational use. Real nutrient content varies by brand, cooking method, soil, and ripeness — treat every number here as a directional estimate, not a lab measurement.
