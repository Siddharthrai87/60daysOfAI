1. Josh Technology Group (JTG) — Associate Software Developer Intern
Overall Risk Score: 48/100
Top Red Flags

Signed security cheque of ₹1,00,000 required as bond collateral (Severity: 8) — You must deposit a signed cheque in the company's name as security against leaving early or not converting to full-time. This is a real, enforceable financial lock-in, not just a verbal commitment.
Misleading headline compensation (Severity: 7) — The "₹12,70,400" total blends your 12-month internship stipend with two years of future, conditional full-time bonuses that are clawed back if you leave early or get a low performance rating. Your actual guaranteed first-year cash is closer to ₹3.3L (₹27,500 × 12), not ₹12.7L.
Internal stack contradiction (Severity: 5) — The company overview boasts Node.js, Go, Flutter, React, AWS — but the actual "Required Skill-Set" for this role lists Java/Ruby/Django preference plus genuinely dated tools (YUI, Dojo, SVN). This mismatch suggests a recycled/templated JD, not necessarily what you'd really work on.
Independent reviews report real overwork (Severity: 6) — Multiple Indeed reviews describe weekend work and an "exhausting" pace, despite the JD's "flexible timings" framing.
Templated copy-paste artifacts (Severity: 3) — "Healthcare" is listed three times in the domains section; this exact JD structure (including the same bond clause and bonus table format) appears in postings from prior years for different cohorts.

Positive Signals

16-year-old, profitable, bootstrapped company — not VC-dependent, lower layoff risk than most startups.
Verifiable track record: 407 Glassdoor reviews give it 3.5/5 stars, with 52% of employees saying they'd recommend it to a friend. Glassdoor
Transparent about work-from-office in Gurugram — no hidden remote bait.
Mentorship is consistently praised in independent reviews, not just the JD's own marketing.
This exact program has run for multiple years with consistent terms — it's a real, recurring hiring pipeline, not a one-off scheme targeting your batch.

2. Better — Associate Software Engineer (Intern), Engineering Internship Program
Overall Risk Score: 60/100
Top Red Flags

Caliber-to-pay mismatch (Severity: 7) — The JD wants candidates who are "already shipping," with real users, OSS commits, or a startup of their own — a senior-caliber bar — for ₹15,000/month and a pre-final-year student.
Vague stack and role detail (Severity: 6) — "Python, React" is the entire technical description. No frameworks, infra, team structure, or concrete responsibilities are specified anywhere in the JD.
Company identity is hard to verify (Severity: 6) — "Better" (bettrsw.com) is a small, real dev/marketing agency, confirmed active on Internshala. But the name collides with an unrelated, much larger US company ("Better Mortgage"/Better.com), which makes independent research unreliable if you're not careful about which "Better" you're checking.
14-month commitment spanning two final semesters with no hours specified (Severity: 6) — Summer internship + 6 months in 7th sem + 6 months in 8th sem, with zero detail on how this is expected to coexist with a full course load.
Informal application process (Severity: 5) — Apply via email with a Loom video, GitHub, and LinkedIn — no listed HR contact, no portal. Not necessarily a scam (early-stage agencies often hire this way), but harder to verify than a structured process.

Positive Signals

Confirmed as a real, currently active company with multiple live internship listings on Internshala, not a single fabricated posting.
States "100% remote" plainly — no bait-and-switch onsite clause buried in fine print.
No bond or financial penalty for leaving — meaningfully lower lock-in risk than JTG.
Gives a concrete PPO number upfront (₹6–7 LPA) rather than vague "competitive salary" language.
The internship-to-PPO model is at least structured and named, not ad hoc.

Bottom line comparing the two: JTG is the more financially risky one (real bond, real cheque) but the more institutionally solid one (16 years, hundreds of verifiable reviews). Better is financially safer (no bond) but operationally murkier (small team, vague role, unverifiable day-to-day) — and asks for a disproportionately high skill bar for the pay. Neither is a scam; both deserve the clarifying questions above before you sign anything.
